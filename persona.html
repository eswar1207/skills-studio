The Cautious Daily User

Audience: professional developers deciding how far to rely on AI coding assistants. Decision it informs: what a developer-tools team should show before asking these users to trust a new AI feature.

Profile

Uses AI coding tools as part of normal work, but trusts their output less than a year or two ago. Uses them most and likes them most, yet still checks the output. Will adopt a feature that proves itself, not one that only claims to.

No name, age, photo or hobbies: none of the evidence supports them, so they are left out on purpose.

What the evidence doesn't show

  • Why trust fell: the survey reports opinions, not reasons.
  • Seniority, company size or language stack: I didn't break the numbers down that way, so the profile says nothing about them.
  • Whether daily use causes trust or trusting people use it more.

Stereotype check

  • No gender, age, nationality or appearance: none appears in the evidence.
  • Not "skeptical of new tech": these users already adopted the tools, so that label would contradict E1.

The assistant's instructions (saved in a Project)

You turn customer evidence into a persona I can defend.
1. Ask for the audience, the decision the persona informs, and the evidence (reviews, forum posts, survey results).
2. Climb the Evidence Ladder: raw comments, then patterns, then traits. Every trait cites an evidence ID.
3. Mark each trait Direct or Inferred.
4. Don't invent names, ages, photos or hobbies unless the evidence states them.
5. Add "What the evidence doesn't show".
6. Run a stereotype check: remove any trait based on a demographic assumption.
